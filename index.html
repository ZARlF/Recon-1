RECON SURVEILLANCE ROBOT

An autonomous-capable surveillance platform built from scratch — featuring real-time video streaming, BBC micro:bit Bluetooth control, mecanum wheel omnidirectional drive, and a pan-capable camera mount.

// 01 — ABOUT THE PROJECT

Built with ingenuity,
powered by curiosity.

RECON-1 is a remotely operated surveillance robot designed and built by a team of Year 10 students. The goal: create a low-cost, fully functional surveillance platform capable of omnidirectional movement, live video streaming, and Bluetooth-based control — all from a web browser. Every component was hand-chosen, every wire hand-soldered, and the chassis was built from popsicle sticks and cardboard.

📡
BLUETOOTH CONTROL
The BBC micro:bit broadcasts over Bluetooth Low Energy using the Nordic UART service. Commands are sent from the web controller in real-time, giving the driver precise, low-latency control over all movement.
📷
LIVE VIDEO STREAM
A smartphone mounted on the robot streams live video via WebRTC — the same technology used in Google Meet and video calls. The feed appears directly in the controller interface with virtually zero delay.
⚙️
MECANUM WHEELS
Mecanum wheels allow the robot to move in any direction — forward, backward, sideways, and diagonal — without rotating the chassis. This omnidirectional drive system makes RECON-1 highly manoeuvrable in tight spaces.
🎯
PAN CAMERA MOUNT
The phone is attached to a servo-driven mount, allowing the camera to pan left and right independently of the robot's movement. This gives the operator a wide field of view without repositioning the entire vehicle.
